How it decides: code first builds a dossier from live data and computes every number (trend, ATR, support and resistance, reward:risk, order-book depth, futures crowding, exchange flows). Then the AI members work in turn: the data analyst reports data quality, evidence and red flags; the news researcher searches the web for delistings, hacks, unlocks and listings (with sources); the bull makes the case for the trade and the bear attacks it; the trader decides as the referee, with deep thinking, under rules that include how often this kind of signal won in the past; and the Final Boss (a stronger model) audits the whole committee on every analysis: hard stops, reward:risk to the nearest resistance, expected value with time-outs, probability adjustments, contradictions and missing data. It answers YES, WATCH (with measurable triggers and an expiry) or NO, with a corrected plan and a position size, and can overturn the trader either way. Code still checks every YES and turns it into WATCH unless: no hard stop (the code's own, a confirmed bad news item, or one the boss listed), the coin is igniting now and has not already run, the plan fits the price, realistic reward:risk (target capped at the nearest resistance) is 2 or more, and expected value after costs is +0.5% or more per trade with the boss's chances held within 15 points of the base rate. Confidence is capped for early signals, demo data and weak markets. If the boss cannot answer, there is no YES. Long only, no leverage, risk at most 1% of your account per trade. This is an AI opinion on data, not financial advice.
